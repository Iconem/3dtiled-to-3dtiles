## Part 1: spherical harmonics above degree 0

### Result so far (2026-11-16)

- **The viewer draws them.** Gaussian Splat Lite reads the harmonics of an SPZ tile: with `sh=1` each tile's mesh
  gets a harmonics texture, with `sh=0` none (checked in headless Chrome on Coit Tower). CesiumJS and Cesium for
  Unreal are still to check.
- **The writer** (`core/spz-glb.js`) stores degrees 1 to 3 in the SPZ payload and declares them in the GLB. Read
  back by another SPZ reader (PlayCanvas splat-transform), the coefficients match within the format's rounding
  (5 bits for degree 1, 4 bits above). Without harmonics a tile is today's, byte for byte.
- **RAD, streamed SOG and LCC2** carry them: `&sh=1|2|3` on `/stream` and `/convert`, `--sh N` offline, and a
  *harmonics* pill in the viewer's top bar for these formats. Capped by the source's own degree. RAD's bands come
  from Spark's decoder (part 2); SOG's from PlayCanvas's reader, which looks each splat's coefficients up in the
  chunk's palette. Without the option the harmonics planes of a SOG chunk are no longer fetched or decoded at all
  (a quarter to a third of a chunk's bytes: the first Skatepark chunk is 6.39 MB of files without them, 8.46 MB
  with its two `shN_*` planes).
- **Extract** passes them through: `/extract/splat` on a tileset asked with `sh=2` gives a PLY with the 24
  coefficient columns (Skatepark, 83 585 splats: 4.7 MB without, 12.7 MB with).
- **No rotation to apply.** None of the readers turns the splats: every format's rotation is in the tileset's root
  transform, which the renderer applies to the coefficients too. So the rotation test of the plan has no case
  today; it becomes needed the day a reader bakes one.
- **GeoSplats has none to carry**: its octants hold the base colour only (means, rotations, scales, `sh0`).
  Checked on the two MapTiler samples of the viewer: 107 970 files listed over their 38 sub-models, and no
  `shN` plane among them.

**Which samples hold harmonics.** The first tile of every streamed-SOG, LCC2 and RAD sample of the viewer, asked
with `sh=3`, and the degree it comes back with (2026-11-16):

| Format | With harmonics | Base colour only |
|---|---|---|
| Streamed SOG | Skatepark, Arrival jaffa3, Grace Cathedral, Cathedral Interior, Vyšehrad, Dittrichova hrobka: degree 3 | Roman Parish, Jastrzębia Góra, Lublin, Itaka Stadium |
| LCC2 | Tunis, Sutro Tower (`.spz` chunks), Bicycle (`.sog` chunks): degree 3 | Unreal street, Unreal statues, Showroom (`.spz`), Unreal example scene (`.ply`) |
| RAD | Coit Tower: degree 1 | Poland village, Cave, Tastier |

So 10 of the 21 samples have something to show with the *harmonics* pill on; on the others it changes nothing.
The first tile grows by 10 % to 53 % with them (Sutro Tower: 2.06 times). The *Oval Interior Spaces* sample was
left out: its host now answers 403, and it was removed from the samples.

**What it costs**, the same tiles asked at each degree, sizes as sent (the payload is gzip):

| Scene | Degree 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coit Tower, RAD (holds degree 1), one 65 536-splat tile | 1 051 KB | +12 % | – | – |
| Skatepark, streamed SOG (degree 3), 6 tiles, 389 257 splats | 15.0 bytes per splat | +10 % | +19 % | +31 % |
| Tunis, LCC2 (degree 3), 4 tiles, 302 022 splats | 14.3 bytes per splat | +11 % | +23 % | +37 % |

- Far below the raw sizes of the table further down (1.5× to 3.4×): SOG stores a palette of coefficient sets, so
  many splats share the same values and gzip finds them.
- **Server time.** RAD: about 1.6 times per tile, the chunk being decoded a second time for the bands. SOG and
  LCC2: the first tiles of a chunk take about 1.3 to 1.4 times as long (its harmonics planes are downloaded and
  decoded too), once per chunk; cutting further tiles from a decoded chunk costs about the same as without. A decoded chunk with harmonics holds up to 45 more columns,
  so the server keeps a quarter as many of them (6 by default).
- Reproduce with `node scripts/measure-splat-sh.mjs <format> <url>` against a running tile server.
- **Parked (2026-11-19):** LCC version 1 harmonics, until a *Quality* scene is at hand to check the unpacking
  against (the public samples are *Portable*, without a `Shcoef.bin`).
- **Left:** the
  look against each format's own viewer by eye, frame time in the viewer, CesiumJS and Cesium for Unreal. The
  offline `--sh` flag was run on the small streamed-SOG test scene only (its tiles declare the coefficients); for
  RAD and LCC2 it is wired and not run.

### Where we are

A Gaussian splat's colour changes with the viewing direction: a base colour (degree 0) plus up to three bands of
spherical-harmonic coefficients (degrees 1 to 3: 3, 8 and 15 coefficients per colour channel). Every splat path
here kept the base colour only when this plan was written:

| Source | What it stores | What we do today |
|---|---|---|
| **Streamed SOG, LCC2** | `shN_centroids.webp` + `shN_labels.webp`: a palette of coefficient sets, one label per splat, 1 to 3 bands | **written to tiles on request** (`sh=`), since 2026-11-16; not downloaded otherwise |
| **LCC** | `Shcoef.bin` in *Quality* scenes: 64 bytes per splat, 15 coefficients packed on 11 bits, degree 3 | not read |
| **RAD** | `sh1`, `sh2`, `sh3` in Spark's packed form | **written to tiles on request** (`sh=`), since 2026-11-16 |
| **GeoSplats** | base colour only in its octants | nothing to carry |
| **SPZ, PLY** (extract, future inputs) | SPZ: quantised coefficients; PLY: `f_rest_*` columns | PLY's are decoded and dropped |

So shiny and view-dependent surfaces look flat. It is the largest remaining gap in splat quality.

### The output side is ready

Tiles are GLBs with `KHR_gaussian_splatting` and its SPZ compression (`core/spz-glb.js`). SPZ has a field for the
degree and stores the coefficients on 8 bits each, and the uncompressed form of the extension reserves
`SH_DEGREE_n_COEF_m` attributes. Nothing new to specify: the writer has to fill what the formats already define.

### What it costs

Coefficients are most of a splat's bytes. Per splat, before gzip:

| Degree kept | Extra bytes | Tile size against today's 19 bytes per splat |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (today) | 0 | 1× |
| 1 | 9 | about 1.5× |
| 2 | 24 | about 2.3× |
| 3 | 45 | about 3.4× |

These are the raw SPZ sizes; gzip narrows the gap, by how much is **to measure**. So the degree is an option, not a
switch: `sh=0|1|2|3` on `/stream` and on the converters, capped by what the source holds.

### Design

- **One option, `sh`**, default to be chosen after the size and speed measurements below (candidates: `0` live and
  the source's degree on export; or `1` everywhere).
- **`core/spz-glb.js`** takes optional coefficient arrays and writes the SPZ degree. One writer serves every format.
- **Each reader** returns coefficients in one layout (per splat, per coefficient, RGB) and one convention, converted
  from its own: SOG's palette looked up, LCC's packed values unpacked and scaled, RAD's unpacked.
- **Rotations.** Coefficients are expressed in the splat's frame. A rotation carried by the tile's transform needs
  nothing: the renderer applies it. A rotation the reader **bakes into positions** (the axis flips some formats get,
  LCC's upright fix) must be applied to the coefficients too, band by band. This is the part most likely to be
  wrong silently, so it has its own test.
- **Coarse levels.** SOG, RAD and LCC store coefficients for their merged, coarse splats too, so levels of detail
  come for free; no averaging of our own.
- **Extract.** `/extract/splat` passes coefficients through when the tiles have them.
- **Clients.** Which viewers draw the coefficients from an SPZ tile (the 3DGS plugin on Gaussian Splat Lite, CesiumJS,
  Cesium for Unreal) is **to check** first: if the main viewer ignores them, the option is only worth its bytes on
  export.

### Phases

| Phase | What | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Check the clients** | one hand-made degree-3 tile, loaded in each viewer | we know who draws it |
| **1. Writer** | `spz-glb.js` writes degrees 1 to 3 | a tile read back with `splat-transform` gives the coefficients within quantisation |
| **2. Streamed SOG** (and LCC2, GeoSplats on the same code) | read the `shN_*` planes when `sh>0` | a SOG scene matches PlayCanvas's own viewer from fixed views |
| **3. RAD** | from the Spark reader of part 2 | matches Spark's viewer |
| **4. LCC** | `Shcoef.bin` | matches the LCC viewer on a *Quality* scene |
| **5. Extract and docs** | pass-through, option documented, sizes published | benchmark table on the [splat tools](/docs/guide/splat-tools) page |

### Tests to do

- **Round trip**: a PLY with known coefficients → tile → read back: equal within the 8-bit step.
- **Rotation**: a synthetic splat lit from one side, pushed through each baked flip; the bright side must stay on
  the same world side. One case per format that bakes a rotation.
- **Convention** (sign and order of coefficients) per format, against that format's own viewer, from four fixed
  views. Splats do not render in our headless Chrome, so this is numbers first, then eyes.
- **Size and speed** per degree on one scene per format: tile bytes raw and gzipped, decode time on the server,
  frame time in the viewer.
- **Sources without coefficients** give exactly today's tiles whatever `sh` says.

## Part 2: RAD through Spark's own Rust reader

### Result (2026-11-16)

Done as planned, in `packages/rad-to-3dtiles`: `rust/spark-rad-wasm` is a thin crate over `spark-lib` at a pinned
commit (Spark 2.4.0), its build is committed in `vendor/spark-rad-wasm` (250 KB), and `src/sparkRad.js` calls it.

| Compared on | Chunks | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Tastier (0.7 M splats) | 6 of 11 | positions, opacity and colour identical; scale and rotation equal to float rounding |
| Cave (World Labs) | 4 | the same |
| Coit Tower (51 M splats, degree-1 harmonics) | 5 | **colours differed by up to 0.145**: a bug of ours, fixed |

- **The bug it found.** A file with spherical harmonics stores base colours outside 0..1 (Coit: -0.015 to 1.145) and
  says so in the property's `min` and `max`. Our reader read the byte as 0..1, so every colour of such a file was
  shifted. It now applies the range, and the two readers agree to 1e-7.
- **Speed.** Spark's decoder takes 0.7 to 0.9 times the time of ours per chunk, single thread.
- **Decision.** Ours stays the reader that makes tiles: it is exact, has no binary, and reads only the properties a
  request needs. Spark's is the reference in the tests (`tile-server/spark-rad.test.js`, a chunk built in the
  file's layout and decoded by both) and on real files (`scripts/compare-rad-readers.mjs`), and it is where the
  spherical-harmonic bands of part 1 will come from: it already returns `sh1` for Coit.
- **Not done:** writing RAD (the encoder is in the crate, not exposed yet); a browser build.

### Where we are

RAD is Spark's streamed splat format. We read it with our own JavaScript port (`radHeader.js`, `lodWalker.js`).
Spark's source has the reference reader and writer in Rust (`rust/spark-lib`, feature `rad`), built for the
browser with `wasm-pack`. The npm package embeds that module with the renderer's exports only, so the RAD
functions are not reachable from it.

### The idea

Build Spark's crate ourselves, once, to WebAssembly for Node, and call it from the RAD package:

```
wasm-pack build --target nodejs --no-default-features --features rad,gsplat
```

then a thin binding from Spark's packed splats to the columns our tiles are written from.

### Why

- **A reference to check against.** Our port was written from reading the format; Spark's code *is* the format.
  Every difference found is a bug on one side.
- **Format changes** arrive by rebuilding, not by re-porting.
- **Spherical harmonics** come decoded by the code that packed them (part 1, phase 3).
- **Writing RAD**, later: the crate has the encoder, so the [one-stop tiler](/docs/guide/planning/one-stop-tiler)
  could build a RAD from a PLY with no Rust toolchain on the user's machine.

### Plan

1. **Build** at a pinned Spark commit; note the exact command, toolchain versions and the crate's licence beside
   the output.
2. **Vendor** the `.wasm` (a few hundred kilobytes, to confirm) and its JavaScript glue in `packages/rad-to-3dtiles`,
   committed. No Rust in CI: a script rebuilds it by hand when Spark changes the format.
3. **Bind**: one function per need (header, hierarchy chunk, node splats), returning typed arrays.
4. **Compare** with our reader on every RAD sample we have: node counts, bounds, and per node the positions,
   scales, rotations, opacities and colours within the format's own quantisation.
5. **Measure** decode time per node and memory, as ratios against the JavaScript reader, single thread and on tile
   workers (each worker loads its own module).
6. **Decide**: Spark's reader as the default with ours as fallback, or ours kept and Spark's used in tests only.
   The comparison and the timings decide; a slower but exact reader is still worth keeping as the test oracle.

About a day for steps 1 to 4.
