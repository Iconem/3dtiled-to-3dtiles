## [Unreleased]

### Changed — 2026-11-12 (a VPC's overview carries the far view; a country no longer opens every tile within 190 km)
- **The overview a VPC names is placed in the mosaic**, as a leaf as large as the set, beside the groups under
  an `ADD` root: its own COPC octree is the view from afar, and the files add theirs as the camera comes in.
  With it, **a file leaf's geometric error is the overview's point spacing** (its points over the set's
  footprint, from a 375-byte header read; the number a COPC node of that spacing declares) instead of the
  file's size. Seen in the viewer over Paris on the France grid: at 19.5 km the overview alone draws (40
  nodes, no file open); at 9.4 km 180 files are open under it (it was every tile in view at any height,
  each reading its COPC root). `&overview=0` leaves it out, `&leafError=<m>` sets the
  error by hand (also for sets without an overview: Canada, Switzerland, USGS Fairbanks have none). A nested
  child is told `&overview=0&leafError=<m>`, so the overview is placed once. `asset.extras.vpc` reports
  `overview: { url, points, spacing, areaKm2, placed }`, `leafError`, `areaKm2`, or `overviewError`.
- `scripts/vpc-split.mjs` writes the items' overview on the root as a `links` entry of `rel: "overview"` (an
  ItemCollection has no assets), and the parser reads one; the France split root on `sample-assets-v1`
  (`france-lidarhd.vpc.gz`) was re-uploaded with it. The VPC docs page says what was weighed (the overview as
  the root's content, one GLB of it, a GLB per group) and why a leaf beside the groups won.
- Checked after the 12 November history rewrite of `main`: the ten rewritten commits (the 13 between
  `255c227` and `4fd8616` kept their hashes) have the same author, dates, message, trees and diffs outside the
  eight purged sample paths, and no purged path exists anywhere in the new history; the Iconem fork is synced
  to `66211bd`. The pre-rewrite tip is kept here as the local branch `backup/main-before-purge-fe7f705`.

### Added — 2026-11-12 (grid VPC: a national campaign as its grid, all of France in 9.5 KB)
- **A grid VPC** (`packages/tile-server/vpc-grid.js`, `"type": "3dtiled:GridVPC"`, `.gridvpc.json[.gz]`, detected as
  `vpc`): the grid (CRS, cell size, the corner a name gives), a URL template with `{x}`, `{y}` and a variant `{v}`,
  and `rows`, one character per square saying whether it exists and under which variant; optionally each present
  square's heights and point count, and `extra` for files whose name is not of the grid. The server expands it in
  memory into the entries a STAC VPC gives and builds the same mosaic (`asset.extras.vpc.boundsFrom: { grid }`).
  **All of IGN's LiDAR HD (337 206 tiles) is 9.5 KB** with the availability alone, 1.5 MB with every tile's
  heights and count, against 30 MB of split STAC VPC in 564 files. Root 23 KB with 34 external subtrees, a subtree
  0.15 s; the first root of a server takes about 30 s (337 206 boxes projected and keyed), 0.1 s after.
- **`scripts/vpc-to-grid.mjs &lt;in.vpc[.gz]> --grid ign-lidarhd|swisstopo --url &lt;template> -o x.gridvpc.json.gz
  [--no-stats]`** writes one from a flat or split VPC, placing each item from its name. The grids
  (`GRIDS`) moved from `copc-to-vpc.mjs` to `vpc-grid.js`, which the script imports.
- **Corrected**: the VPC page said a grid template over IGN "404s on a quarter of its cells" and concluded the
  grid could not describe the set. The observation stands (flai names a square `_PTS_C_` or `_PTS_O_`, 258 474 and
  78 716, never both), the conclusion did not: one character per square says which, and a missing square is
  simply not listed. The page now says so.
- Library: `france-lidarhd` is now the grid VPC with heights and counts; `france-lidarhd-grid` the 9.5 KB
  availability-only one; `france-lidarhd-stac` the split STAC VPC.

### Changed — 2026-11-12 (streamed SOG: the tree collapsed per LOD level, offline and live)
- **`sog-to-3dtiles --splat-budget N`** (default 100 000; `0` keeps one tile per leaf run): the tiles of a LOD level
  are the shallowest nodes whose splats of that level fit the budget, each holding the runs of all its leaves; a
  tile of level L has as children the tiles of level L−1 under it. Made for Andrii Shramko's Lublin scan, whose
  1:1 tree drew nothing in cesium-native until 126 672 tiles had loaded.
- **The live route collapses the same way** (`collapseLodTree` in `sog-to-3dtiles/src/lodMeta.js`, shared):
  `&splatBudget=N`, `TILED_SOG_SPLAT_BUDGET`. A merged tile is `tile/m<path>_<level>.glb`, its node and its level,
  and the server collects the runs under it. Lublin live (259 M gaussians at level 0, 518 M over 13 levels):
  **7 169 tiles and a 2.3 MB `tileset.json`, was 117 689 tiles and 40.9 MB**; median 65 000 splats a tile, one or
  two chunk files each (32 at most); the root tile is the whole scene at the coarsest level, 1 MB in 7.6 s cold.
  `lodErrors` is honoured by the 1:1 mapping only.
- Library: **Lublin, Poland** (259 M, CC BY, the scene PlayCanvas showed at the Metaverse Standards Forum town
  hall) and **Itaka Stadium, Opole** (212 M, CC BY), both by Andrii Shramko on SuperSplat's CDN.
- Docs: `KHR_gaussian_splatting` is marked **ratified** (it said release candidate), with a table of the
  extensions built on it, every one still an open pull request on 2026-11-12: `_compression_spz_2` (#2531),
  `_compression_lgsc` (#2551), `_compression_EGSC` (#2605), `MPEG_gaussian_splatting_transport` (#2573),
  `_wide_gamut_color` (#2539).

### Researched — 2026-11-11 (what was looked into and not built, and why)
- **OSGB in the 100 M-vertex range and above**: the only public one found is Hong Kong's Planning Department model
  (2 150 zipped tiles, 467 GB, about 10 G vertices, 4.7 M a tile on average, 6.4 M in the Central tile), now in

  tiled format with levels of detail that has large public scenes (the 259 M one above); SOG v3 shipped a week

  usd-wg assets are too small to need tiles; only Disney's Moana Island (about 90 M polygons, 28 M instances,

- **I3S point clouds** were already supported (three library entries). **Open samples of other formats**: Hexagon
  HSPC, Metashape TLS, Nearmap and Vexcel publish no native sample and export 3D Tiles; Skyline 3DML has one

  the France sets are built from flai's COPC mirror and its listing of 2026-11-10.

### Added — 2026-11-11 (VPC of VPCs: a country as a hierarchy of VPC files, and four more countries' worth)
- **`scripts/vpc-split.mjs <in.vpc[.gz]> -o <dir>/<name>.vpc.gz [--max-items 2048] [--max-bytes 2000000]`** cuts a
  VPC into a quadtree of child VPC files, each under the item count and the JSON size (Potree's hierarchy pages,
  for VPCs), and writes a root whose items stand for the children: `pc:type: "vpc"`, the child file as the data
  asset, `vpc:items`, the sum of `pc:count`, and the union of the items' `bbox` / `geometry` / `proj:bbox` (the
  native box when they share one CRS); the same children as `links` of `rel: child`, each child linking back to
  its parent. The root lists the leaves directly when it fits the limits itself; intermediate levels only when
  not. Quadkeys 0 SW, 1 SE, 2 NW, 3 NE from the root's extent. All of France: 337 206 items → a 60 KB root
  over 563 files of 9–1 200 items (each with its WKT), 48 s.
- **Nested VPCs are served**: an item whose asset is a `.vpc` / `.vpc.gz` (or says `pc:type: "vpc"`), and a
  root-level child link, become `format: vpc` entries of the mosaic whose leaf content is this server's own
  mosaic of that child (`/stream/tileset.json?format=vpc&url=<child>`), placed from the item's `proj:bbox`
  like any other: the root mosaic is built from the root file alone and a child is read only when a client
  refines into it, where it is cut every four group levels past 1 024 items as any VPC. A child named only by a
  link is placed by reading its mosaic root. `asset.extras.vpc.nested` counts them; `index.json` lists them as
  `vpc` rows. The tile-server also serves its committed samples read-only at `/samples/<name>`, so a split set
  opens locally as `/aggregate/tileset.json?vpc=http://localhost:<port>/samples/<name>.vpc.gz` with the
  children resolved relative to it. Walked end to end on each set below: root (10–293 KB, 0.2–0.7 s) → child
  mosaic (19–317 KB, 0.2–0.3 s, cut when over 1 024 items) → subtree (27–29 ms) → COPC root (0.8–1.4 s from S3) →
  first tile (0.7–1.2 MB, 0.9–1.8 s).
- **Four more sets in the library, split**: **all of IGN's LiDAR HD** (`france-lidarhd.vpc.gz`, the 337 206
  `.copc.laz` tiles flai mirrors, every header read in about 70 minutes over three runs, 10 071 G points, Lambert-93 WKT per item,
  563 children, 30 MB gzipped in all under a 60 KB root), **swissSURFACE3D** (`switzerland-swisssurface3d.vpc.gz`:
  the 15 643 of swisstopo's 58 987 STAC items served as COPC on 2026-11-10, 514.8 G points, LV95 WKT, 27
  children), **CanElevation New Brunswick + Prince Edward Island** (`canada-canelevation-nb-pei.vpc.gz`: every
  COPC under `NB/` and `PEI/` of NRCan's bucket, 90 764 of its 751 929 tiles, 39 projects, 1 733 G points,
  NAD83(CSRS) UTM 19N / 20N WKT, 147 children, 9.9 MB gzipped in all) and a **USGS 3DEP project through NOAA's
  Digital Coast** (`usgs-3dep-fairbanks-2017.vpc.gz`: Fairbanks North Star Borough 2017 QL1 + QL2, dataset
  8663, 7 471 tiles, 63.0 G points, 15 children). USGS's own 3DEP is EPT (public) or COPC behind signed URLs,
  so NOAA's plain-COPC copies are what a static VPC can point at. Every header of the four was read.
- **`scripts/copc-to-vpc.mjs` for a country**: `--cache headers.ndjson` keeps every header read as it lands and
  resumes a killed run (France's and New Brunswick's each took several runs); the FeatureCollection is streamed
  out feature by feature, and `vpc-split` scans one in the same way, since all of France (557 MB of JSON) is more
  than one V8 string holds; `--listing keys.tsv` keeps the
  bucket listing (`--prefix` may repeat); `--concurrency N`; `--estimate <grid>` writes a country in two
  minutes from the listing alone, the footprint from the tile name (grids: `ign-lidarhd`, `swisstopo`) and
  `pc:count` from the file size through a bytes-per-point ratio fitted on `--calibrate N` real headers (IGN:
  5.609 B/point over 3 000 files, a count within 10.4 % at the median, 28 % at p90), heights from the nearest
  calibrated tiles, the items marked `3dtiled:estimated`; `--grid <grid> --extent … --url <template>` writes
  every cell of an extent with nothing read, a cell with no file 404ing at the leaf. Found on the way: IGN's
  tile names end in `_PTS_C_` (258 474) or `_PTS_O_` (78 716) and the grid does not say which, so a URL
  template over France misses a quarter of the tiles; the listing is what tells.
- 7 tests: the split (the union item; under the limits nothing is split; a 4 900-tile grid into leaves each item
  reaches once, the root's items and links naming every file; the byte limit and intermediate levels; items
  without a box kept) and the serving (a root of child items and links parsed to nested entries with bounds;
  a split set served through `/stream` from the root file alone, the leaf its child's mosaic, the child read
  once, `index.json` listing `vpc` rows).
### Changed — 2026-11-11 (SOG v3: splat-transform 3.11, opacity above 1, mixed-version streamed SOG)
- **SOG v3 is read**, live and offline, next to v2: `@playcanvas/splat-transform` ^3.11.1 (was 3.10.3) in
  `sog-to-3dtiles` and `tile-server`. Version 3 ([#370](https://github.com/playcanvas/splat-transform/pull/370),
  the default of splat-transform's CLI from 3.11 and what SuperSplat will publish) drops `quats.webp` for a 24-bit
  exponential-map rotation in the alpha channels of `means_l`/`means_u`/`scales`, stores opacity as `(b / 204)²` up
  to 1.5625 (`meta.opacity.max` when some exceed 1), labels its SH palette on 24 bits and allows a 4th SH band. None
  of that reaches our reader: `readSog` gives the same columns and row order for both. A streamed SOG's
  `lod-meta.json` stays version 1 and its chunks may mix v2 and v3; both routes take them chunk by chunk.
- **Opacity above 1 is clamped to 1 in the tile.** The DataTable's `opacity` logit saturates (`+Infinity` for 1,
  16.64 above it) and the true value comes as `opacity_linear`; `sogChunk.js`'s `extractRange` takes that column when
  it is there and clamps, since SPZ v2 holds one alpha byte of [0, 1] and this repo's viewers draw no opaque core.
  So a v3 chunk gives the tile its v2 encoding would (v2 clamps on write). The tile worker hands the column over
  too, and the GeoSplats and worker paths of `splat-tile.js` now go through the same `extractRange`.
- **`lodErrors`**: when a `lod-meta.json` says so, a leaf's `errors` (one per LOD level, relative magnitudes) set
  the geometric errors of its LOD chain: the coarsest level keeps its depth's error, each finer one its share, the
  finest refining to 0 (`sog-to-3dtiles/src/lodMeta.js`, shared by the converter and `sog-live.js`). Halving stays
  for a leaf without usable errors.
- Fixtures (`packages/sog-to-3dtiles/test/fixtures`, 829 KB, from `scripts/make-fixtures.mjs` and the seeded scene
  of `test/scene.mjs`, 3 000 splats with one SH band): the scene as SOG v2 and v3, and as a streamed SOG of three
  LOD levels in each version, whose decimated level holds opacity above 1 (`opacity.max` 1.05). 5 tests
  (`pnpm --filter sog-to-3dtiles test`): v2 and v3 decode alike (positions within 1e-4, colours within a codebook
  step, alphas within 0.01, rotations within 2.5°) and are the scene; the over-unity chunk clamps and its SPZ
  positions equal the v2 chunk's; a scene mixing v2 and v3 chunks converts offline and serves live; `lodErrors`.
- Checked end to end on the Skatepark preset through `/stream/tileset.json?format=sog`: the tileset (2 054 tiles,
  516 leaves) in 0.7 s, a 125-splat leaf GLB in 12.7 s with its chunk decoded cold, SPZ v2 payload and GLB chunk
  lengths as expected. The tile-server and core suites stay at 194 passing (one lcc2 expectation updated: a SOG
  written by 3.11 has no `quats.webp`); the viewer type-checks.
### Changed — 2026-11-11 (range reads of Potree 2.0, LCC, LCC2 and Nexus coalesced, one getter per source URL)
- **The Potree 2.0, LCC, LCC2 and Nexus live adapters read through the coalescing range getter** that COPC has used
  since 2026-10-29 (`packages/tile-server/range-getter.js`): reads of one file issued in the same tick are sorted by
  offset and merged when less than 64 KB apart, 8 MB at most a request, each caller given its own bytes. The getter
  is now **one per source URL, shared across tile requests** (`sharedRangeGetter`, an LRU of 256 URLs; `readRange`
  is the adapters' call), so a viewer's burst of sibling tiles, which these formats lay out back-to-back (Potree's
  `octree.bin` and `hierarchy.bin`, Nexus nodes and textures, LCC's `data.bin`, the planes of a bundled SOG chunk),
  is a few merged requests. Error semantics are unchanged: a merged request that fails rejects each of its reads with
  the host's error (`status`, `transient`), the per-host cap and retries of `core/source-fetch.js` apply to the
  merged request. A Nexus texture kept in the cache is copied out of the merged read so the cache does not pin it.
- `TILED_RANGE_COALESCE_MS=<ms>` (or `windowMs`) widens the batching window from one tick (`setImmediate`, the
  default, COPC's behaviour unchanged) to that many milliseconds, so that tile requests a few milliseconds apart
  merge too, at the cost of that latency on every read. `RANGE_DEBUG=1` logs each flush (reads → requests, bytes
  asked → fetched) and `rangeStats` counts them.
- Measured through the server (one client, a tileset and every tile of its first levels with 16 in flight, the public
  hosts; the table and the method in the architecture guide): the Potree lion's hierarchy drain goes from 422
  requests to 29 for the same 47 KB; 40 lion tiles from 40 requests to 26 (18 with a 20 ms window); 23 gargoyle
  nodes with their textures from 23 to 12 (3 with 20 ms), 91 from 91 to 74 (22); LCC2 Tunis stays at 6 requests
  for 30 tiles (a chunk is already a tail read plus one span per run of kept planes). Bytes read are the same
  (+1.3 % read through gaps on 155 lion tiles with a window) and wall time does not move against these hosts, so
  the default window stays at 0.
- Tests: `range-getter.test.js` (4): merging across gaps up to `gap` and the `maxBytes` cap, each read its own bytes
  and an invalid range refused; a failed merged request rejecting each read with the host's error; the shared
  per-URL getter, its keys and counters; the batching window against the one-tick default. The Nexus stream test
  now expects a node and its two textures as one merged read.

### Added — 2026-11-11 (OSGB at city scale: Hong Kong's 2 150 zipped tiles, read in place)
- **A city-scale OSGB dataset in the library**: the Hong Kong Planning Department's 3D Photo-realistic Model
  (data.gov.hk, free commercial and non-commercial reuse), Kowloon and Hong Kong Island in 2 150 tiles of 250 m,
  each one zip on `pdmap.pland.gov.hk`, **467.2 GB in all** (a `HEAD` of every zip; 0 to 1 394 MB each) and, from
  three tiles measured, of the order of 10 G vertices in some 700 000 files. Two families: 822 ContextCapture tiles
  over Kowloon (version 91, 12 levels, 101 GB) and 1 328 OSG-converted tiles over the island (version 97, an exact
  quadtree of 1 024 leaves, 366 GB), the family of the tile 21-25 already in the library. Two pills: the whole set by
  its `GridIdx_OSGB.csv` index, and one Mong Kok tile. The answer to "nothing in the 100 M range?": the only
  city-scale OSGB found in the open; every other open city mesh (LandsD's own, Helsinki, Melbourne, Estonia, Taiwan)
  is OBJ, 3D Tiles or I3S, and the big OSGB "shares" on CSDN are Google Earth re-exports on Baidu pan.
- **Datasets in zip archives are served without unpacking** (`url=…/tile.zip`): `core/zip.js` reads an archive
  member by member, over range requests or from disk, from its central directory (zip64 included); each `.osgb` is
  one range read, inflated in memory. `core/source-fetch.js` falls back to Node's lenient HTTP parser for a host
  whose answers `fetch` refuses (pdmap folds a long header over two lines), from the first refusal on.
- **A CSV index of zips is a collection**: one tileset whose children are the zips, each an external tileset placed
  by its extent (WGS84 columns, or projected ones with `&srs=` on the URL) and opened when the camera comes within
  twice the tile's width. `?srs=EPSG:2326&origin=x,y,z` on a dataset URL stand in for a missing `metadata.xml`.
  `_OSGB.zip` and `GridIdx_OSGB.csv` names are detected as OSGB.
- Measured through the server: a zip's root tileset in 3.0 s, an L22 leaf in 14 s, root to L17 in 49 s; the
  collection of 2 150 in 35 s (694 KB, 9 levels of groups). `scripts/osgb-stats.mjs` crawls a zip (`--max-files`,
  `--max-depth` for a big one, the unread files counted per depth): `Tile_+005_+006` 873 354 vertices, 1 187 533
  triangles, 309 tiles, 96 leaves, 12 levels; `tile_16_11` 3 234 618 vertices, 1 365 tiles, 1 024 leaves; `tile_18_27` (Central) 6 414 429 vertices, 2 138 143 triangles, the densest of the three (442.6 MB of files in a 332 MB zip).
  The host gives about 650 KB/s a connection and drops requests under many: 156 s, 802 s (4 DNS failures, re-run
  from the cache in 25 s) and 922 s for the three.
- 6 tests: the lenient client on a server that folds its headers; zip members by range and from a file, deflated and
  stored; a zipped dataset served member by member; the placement hints and their yielding to a `metadata.xml`; a
  tile index as a collection (the Planning Department's UTF-16 columns), its hints passed to each tile; osgb-stats
  on a zip, whole and cut by `--max-files` / `--max-depth`.

### Added — 2026-11-11 (Nexus: 3DHOP's multiresolution meshes, live and offline)
- **Nexus (`.nxs` / `.nxz`, CNR-ISTI Visual Computing Lab, the format of 3DHOP) is read, live and offline.**
  `GET /stream/tileset.json?format=nexus&url=<….nxz>` (`packages/tile-server/nexus-live.js`) builds the tileset
  from two range reads (the 88-byte header, then the node / patch / texture tables) and serves one node per tile,
  its bytes and the JPEGs its patches name read by range and written as a GLB (positions, int16 normals made unit,
  RGBA colours, uvs with v flipped, one primitive per texture); `3dtiled <file.nxs|nxz> <outDir>` converts the same
  way offline (`packages/nexus-to-3dtiles/`, `--dag`, `--ge-scale`, `--workers`). The reader is written from the
  layout alone; the compressed nodes of an `.nxz` are decoded by the MIT JavaScript decoders of Corto and of Nexus's
  own loader (meco), vendored unchanged with their licences, so nothing GPL is linked. Detected from the extension
  (`core/detect.js`), in `openapi.json`, on the server banner, tile workers through `tile-tasks-mesh.js`.
- **A DAG becomes a tree two ways** (`nexus-tileset.js`). A Nexus file is a DAG: a finer node is named by the
  patches of several coarser nodes (171 of the gargoyle's 187, up to 10 parents), and the viewer draws it once. By
  default (`dag=levels`) the DAG is cut by level, each level being a complete mesh at one resolution: a chain of
  contentless level tiles, under each the level's nodes as content tiles sharing one sphere and one geometric
  error so that a client switches them together, each node culled by its own sphere as `content.boundingVolume`
  and vanishing through an empty child. `dag=paths` writes a shared node under each parent, one tile per
  root-to-node path with the same GLB (130 for the shell, 25 202 for the gargoyle, 76 million for the bust), with
  Nexus's per-node choice of level and its duplicated draws. `geometricError = 8 × node error` (`ge=`,
  `--ge-scale`): Nexus refines at 2 px of error, a 3D Tiles client at 16 px, the same metric.
- **Four public `.nxz` files found and read in full**, every host answering `Range`: 3DHOP's gargoyle (7.3 MB,
  187 nodes in 9 levels, 3 966 820 triangles, normals and colours), and from the `JulyTheMonth/NexusDataset`
  Hugging Face dataset the Smithsonian's Unio hainesianus shell (40 MB, 28 nodes, 541 840 triangles, 28 JPEGs,
  CC0), the Stanford dragon (3.5 MB, 85 nodes, 1 735 694 triangles) and the Smithsonian's George Washington bust
  (242 MB, 2 541 nodes in 13 levels, 52 116 066 triangles, 2 541 JPEGs, CC0). All four in the viewer's new Nexus
  pill. Measured: the gargoyle's tileset in 5.8 s and its tiles (150 to 580 KB) in 0.5 to 1.1 s from GitHub's raw

### Added — 2026-11-09 (VPC: a PDAL / QGIS virtual point cloud as one tileset)
- **`/stream/tileset.json?format=vpc&url=<….vpc>`** serves a virtual point cloud, the STAC ItemCollection PDAL
  and QGIS write to treat a folder of LAS/LAZ/COPC tiles as one dataset, as a single mosaic whose leaves are the
  COPC streams of its items. `.vpc` is detected from the extension. The same mosaic is
  `/aggregate/tileset.json?vpc=<url>`: a VPC is parsed into the set manifest `/aggregate` already serves, so
  nesting, selection, `index.json` and `&cache=` all apply.
- **The root is built from the `.vpc` alone.** Each leaf is placed from its item's `proj:bbox`, reprojected to
  ECEF from `proj:wkt2` / `proj:projjson` / `proj:epsg` (each item may have its own CRS), then the WGS 84
  `bbox`, then the `geometry`. Measured: 1 000 tiles of Luxembourg's 2019 LiDAR (7.0 G points, a 1.06 MB
  `.vpc`) give a 643 KB root in 161 ms, 138 groups 8 deep, and no COPC file opened; the 9 AHN5 tiles of
  HPC-DAT/copc-dataset (227 M points) a 6.5 KB root. Against the COPC streams' own roots: centres 0.00 m apart
  on AHN5, geometric errors equal to the metre (1732.19 against 1732.0).
- **Only COPC items are streamed.** A plain LAS/LAZ item is skipped, like EPT and local paths, and counted with
  its reason in `asset.extras.vpc`, in `X-VPC-Streamed` / `X-VPC-Skipped` / `X-VPC-Skipped-Reasons` and in
  the log; a VPC with no COPC item is a 422 that says so. An item named `.laz` may be a COPC: one 589-byte
  read tells (the first 16, then all of them only if one was; `&sniff=all|0`).
- **The `overview` asset is reported, not drawn**: `asset.extras.vpc.overviews` gives its COPC stream. A tile
  whose content is an external tileset may not have children, so it cannot be the root's content over the
  leaves; the docs page says what the workaround would cost.
- A COPC that declares no CRS takes the item's EPSG code (`&crs=`, read from `proj:epsg` or from inside the
  WKT). WKT2 `COMPOUNDCRS` and PROJJSON compound CRSs are read through their horizontal part, as `COMPD_CS` was.
- `scripts/copc-to-vpc.mjs` writes a VPC over online COPC files without PDAL (one 64 KB read per file: 35
  files in 4.3 s, 1 000 in 107 s); `scripts/vpc-to-manifest.mjs` turns a VPC into a set manifest with baked
  bounds. `packages/tile-server/samples/dublin-city-2015.vpc` is self-made with the first: 35 tiles of NYU's
  2015 Dublin scan from flai's mirror, 1.42 G points, with its overview.
- Viewer: a **VPC** format with AHN5 Amsterdam, QGIS's Sunshine Coast test set and the Dublin sample. Docs: a
  VPC page under references, and sections in the formats and meta-tilesets guides. Not done: offline
  conversion of a whole VPC (refused with a message), plain LAS/LAZ and EPT items, local paths.
### Added — 2026-11-09 (OSGB: OpenSceneGraph tiled meshes, streamed)
- **A new input format, OSGB**, what ContextCapture / iTwin Capture, DJI Terra and Smart3D write as an
  "OpenSceneGraph" tiled model: `GET /stream/tileset.json?format=osgb&url=<…/metadata.xml | folder | root .osgb>`,
  from an http(s) URL or a local folder. Each `.osgb` is one level of one tile and names the file that refines it,
  so the tree is served as 3MX's is, one lazy external-tileset fragment per file (`/stream/osgb/node/…`). Detected
  from a URL ending in `metadata.xml` or `.osgb`. Reference:
  [OSGB](apps/docs/content/docs/references/mesh-generic/osgb.mdx).
- **A reader of OpenSceneGraph's binary scene files in JavaScript** (`packages/osgb-to-3dtiles`, no OSG library, no
  native code), written from the layout of the format, which has no specification: file versions 80 (OSG 3.0) to
  161 (3.6), with or without block sizes, zlib bodies, arrays inline or as objects. Checked on **3 918 real files of
  8 datasets** (14.7 million vertices, versions 80, 91, 97 and 131) and 4 generic OSG models: every file reads in
  full.
- **Geometric error from the PagedLOD's pixel range**: `2 · radius · 16 / range`, which makes a 3D Tiles client at
  its default 16 px switch levels at the distance OpenSceneGraph does. Bounding volumes are OSG's own spheres. The
  error never grows from a tile to its children on any dataset (7 600 tiles checked), and every mesh is inside its
  tile's sphere.
- **Georeference from `metadata.xml`**: `ENU:lat,lon`, a projected `EPSG:n` with its origin, or `EPSG:4326` with a
  longitude/latitude origin (the form of the DJI Terra layout). core/geo.js now derives the CGCS2000 Gauss-Krüger
  zones (EPSG:4491 to 4554) most Chinese exports are in; a code it does not know is asked from epsg.io once.
  Dayanta lands on the Giant Wild Goose Pagoda in Xi'an (34.2196° N, 108.9590° E), the Hong Kong tile on Central.
- **Textures**: the JPEG embedded in every photogrammetry export passes through untouched (6 867 of them in the
  eight datasets). DDS files and raw S3TC blocks (DXT1, 3, 5) and raw RGB(A) are decoded and re-encoded in
  JavaScript; an untextured mesh keeps the grey of its OSG material.
- **Top tiles without a directory listing**: a local folder is listed, a GitHub raw URL through one tree API call,
  an HTML index is read, and otherwise the exporters' names are probed (`Data/Tile_+xxx_+yyy/…` outwards from the
  tiles found, `Block/Block.osgb`, `Data/Model/Model.osgb`). A finer file a copy lacks is answered with its
  parent's mesh, not a hole: Dayanta's public copy stops at level 17.
- **Measured**: a tile is built in 1.3 to 4.2 ms and a fragment in 0.7 to 1.9 ms from disk. Weinan (2 641 files,
  183 MB on GitHub) opens in 2.0 to 3.1 s, the read of its 30 root files, and its whole tree is 3 701 tiles, 199 MB
  of GLB and 4.4 million triangles. `3dtiled export` of a streamed OSGB tileset writes it as static 3D Tiles.
- **Viewer**: an OSGB pill with four public datasets (Weinan, Dayanta, a Hong Kong tile, a one-tile sample);
  23 tests, on files written by a small OSGB encoder so that none needs the network.
- **Not there**: no offline converter (`/convert`); `&transform=` does not reach the tiles; classes outside the
  mesh ones (`ProxyNode`, `Billboard`, `osgSim`…) stop the read of a file that has no block sizes; a zlib body, a
  DDS file and OSG 3.6 files were not found in any real dataset and are covered by synthetic files only.

### Added — 2026-11-05 (tile workers, behind a flag)

  unless it is set. With it, the live server's COPC and Potree tiles (1.x and 2.0, `/stream` and crawl exports) and the
  offline `copc-to-3dtiles` and `potree-to-3dtiles` converters (also `--workers <n>`) hand each tile to a pool of
  `worker_threads`: the server's thread only fetches the bytes, a worker runs the LAZ or brotli decode and writes the GLB.
  `auto` is one worker per core but one, at most 8. Tiles are byte for byte the same either way (a test checks it).
- **Measured on the 10.6 million point Autzen COPC (278 tiles, local source, 20 cores):** every tile through `/stream`
  in 13.9 s on one thread and 3.5 s with 8 workers; the offline converter 15.7 s and 5.3 s. 4 workers already give
  2.6 to 2.9 times, and more than 8 gave nothing. While the single thread worked, another request to the server waited
  up to 1.8 s; with workers, 10 ms. The cost is memory: the server went from about 270 MB to about 680 MB with 8 workers.
- **The desktop app's Export card has it under Advanced, "Tile threads":** one thread (default), auto, 2, 4 or 8. It
  applies to that export only, crawl or offline converter, through `workers` on `POST /export` and `--workers` on the
  `3dtiled` CLI. A crawl run as a CLI subprocess reads tiles the tile-server builds, so the choice is greyed out there.
- **The desktop app's Build tab has a tab per flow**, "Stream live" and "Export to a folder", under the dataset
  form, instead of one card under the other. An export carries on while the other tab is open.
- **A tile the workers have no memory for is not lost.** If a worker dies or an allocation fails in it, that tile is
  built on the main thread instead, one at a time: straight away on the server, after all the others in the converters.
  A warning says how many, and that fewer workers would avoid it. A tile that fails for its own data still fails.
- **Every streamed format has it on the live server:** streamed SOG, LCC, RAD, GeoSplats, I3S and 3MX as well as
  COPC and Potree, so `/stream` and crawl exports of all of them. For SOG the chunk's WebP decode and each tile's
  encode run on workers while the decoded chunk stays cached on the main thread. Checked on two samples of most
  formats: tiles are byte for byte those of the previous release, with the flag off and with 4 workers. The gain is
  smaller than for point clouds, since splat tiles already compressed off the main thread and these sources are
  slower to fetch than to decode: 1.6 times on a streamed SOG, 1.35 on an I3S mesh, about 1.1 on RAD and LCC.
- Still on one thread: the offline converters other than COPC and Potree, and plain LAS/LAZ. A curved COPC placement
  is rebuilt in each worker.

### Fixed — 2026-11-04 (Potree 1.x lost most of a cloud's finest points)
- **Potree 1.x leaves counted as 0 points were dropped.** PotreeConverter writes a count of 0 in the `.hrc` for a leaf
  whose `.bin` holds thousands of points: a leaf's points go to its `store`, and only an inner node adds to `numAccepted`. `/stream`, crawl exports and the offline converter all skipped them. On a
  1.3 billion point Iconem cloud that was 169,134 of 209,474 nodes, about 3/4 of the points, all at the fine end. Potree
  itself reads any listed node, which is why it showed more detail. Re-export any Potree 1.x cloud exported before this.
- **The offline converter on a remote Potree 1.x cloud lost the same points**: its mirror had its own copy of the filter
  and never downloaded those files. `/convert` caches from before this are not reused.
- **A failed `.hrc` read no longer loses a branch.** It counted as "no such chunk", and the server remembered the chunk
  as loaded, so one failed request dropped that branch until a restart (8 of 493 nodes, 1% of the points, in one test).
  Reads now retry, and a read that still fails is an error the next request retries.
- **One read layer for every source format** (`core/source-fetch.js`): retries, a cap per host, errors that keep the host's
  status. SOG, LCC, RAD, I3S and 3MX had no retries: one 503 on their first file failed the dataset until a restart, as a
  failed first read was remembered for good. It now expires after a minute, on all of them. Exported through a host failing
  every request once, COPC, Potree 1 and 2, SOG, LCC, RAD, I3S and plain 3D Tiles come out identical to a clean export.
- **`/stream` says why a read failed:** 502 if the host did not answer, 404 if the file is not there. A test runs these rules
  against every format. GeoSplats, `.3dtiles` and `.3tz` packages pass the same export check as the others.
- **Point tiles can carry the LAS dimensions** as glTF custom attributes: `/stream` with `&attributes=` naming them by
  their PDAL names in any case (`Intensity,Classification,ReturnNumber,GpsTime`, …, or `all`), from COPC (every
  dimension the file has; GpsTime float32 relative to the tile, offset in the primitive's extras) and Potree 1.x/2.0
  (intensity and classification). The offline converters always write `_INTENSITY` and `_CLASSIFICATION`. Off by default on
  `/stream`, so a tile stays as light as its points and colours (each adds 4 bytes a point, glTF-aligned: +40% on an Autzen tile). Nothing was sent
  before: a cloud without RGB had only white `COLOR_0`.
- **A crawl export of a deep implicit tree shows progress and starts at once.** Reading the availability of a 25-level
  Potree cloud (the 1.5 billion point Venice scan) walked tens of thousands of subtrees before the first download and
  reported nothing meanwhile, so the desktop app sat on "Reading the tree" with an empty bar for minutes. The walk now
  counts the subtrees read, and a copy that keeps implicit tiling downloads each subtree and its tiles as soon as that
  subtree is read. The walk reads 8 subtrees at a time instead of every child at once, so the server that generates them
  keeps answering the viewer. Cancel stops the walk, not only the downloads after it.
- **Desktop: the file and folder pickers work.** Electrobun's `openFileDialog` never answers when called from an RPC
  request handler (blackboardsh/electrobun#156: its synchronous native call blocks the loop the answer travels on), and
  the view gave up after 1 s anyway ("RPC request timed out"). On Windows both pickers are now Windows' own dialogs,
  and the view waits as long as the user does.
- **Desktop: Export becomes a red, outlined Cancel while a run is on**; the Advanced options sit two to a row; the
  header pill probes the tile-server every 10 s and says when it stops answering; after an export, **Copy file:// URL
  for Unreal** gives the `file:///C:/…/tileset.json` URL Cesium for Unreal's URL source takes.
- **Web viewer: copy buttons** next to Load for the tileset URL being loaded (the middleware output, absolute) and the
  raw source URL behind it.
- **Desktop: the Advanced rows wrap** instead of squeezing five selects into one line.
- **Desktop: the servers the app starts exit with it.** Windows keeps a child running when its parent is killed or
  crashes; the tile-server of a dev build stayed up after the app was gone, and the next launch adopted it, running that
  old code. Both servers now follow the app's process, and closing the window stops them.
- **The cap on requests to one host adapts:** it halves when the host fails under load (a 429, a 503, a reset) and climbs
  back as reads pass, between 4 and the configured 32.
- **The tile-server listens in 0.5 s instead of 10–15 s:** the format adapters and the tools load after the port is bound.
- **Desktop: the window opens at once**, in its "starting the tile-server" state, and reloads with the ports when the servers
  are up. It used to wait for them, showing nothing for 15–20 s, and up to two minutes when a port probe hung.
- **Desktop: the view is React + Tailwind + shadcn/ui on Base UI**, built by Vite; same layout, texts and theme.
- **Windows Defender may quarantine the app's `launcher.exe`** (`Trojan:Win32/Bearfoos.A!ml`, a false positive on the unsigned
  Electrobun launcher, first seen 2026-11-04): the Start-menu shortcut then does nothing, an update fails while applying,
  and the installer fails with `AntivirusInterference`. See the desktop guide for the exclusion to add until the app is signed.
- **I3S: a node page that failed ended the pages**, and every node after it was lost until a restart. It now fails the
  request, and the next one resumes from that page.
- **I3S: a tile whose geometry could not be read was served as an empty tile**, and one whose texture or colours could not
  be read lost them, with a 200. An export now also asks for the whole tree when a huge layer was still loading.
- **LCC: a host that ignores `Range` got wrong splats**, read from the start of `data.bin`.
- **COPC: a CRS that could not be read left the dataset unplaced until a restart.** A file deeper than level 8 whose root
  page does not list the depth is no longer cut off there.
- **Offline converter: LCC's `index.bin` and `data.bin` could fail unnoticed.** Every mirror now retries.
- **An export of a mosaic that lost a child now says so**, under `hierarchyErrors`.
- **A failed hierarchy read reached clients as an empty file**, for every octree format: `/stream` sent its 200 before
  reading the subtree, so an error became an empty body.
- **A crawl pruned any subtree it could not read**, with no error. An export now stops on it, or with
  `hierarchyErrors: 'warn'` (`--hierarchy-errors warn`) lists it and carries on.
- **Potree 2: a block near the root could fire hundreds of range reads at once**, unbounded and without retries. On
  `lion` from potree.org, 25 of 116 subtrees failed and a client loaded 1,149 of its 1,624 nodes.
- **Potree 1.x: a subtree built while a chunk was in flight came out without it**, and a walk stopped at any chunk
  already loaded, missing those below it.

### Fixed — 2026-10-31 (data stopped refining: the globe filled the shared tile cache)
- Measured, a globe tile holds a 512² RGBA imagery texture (1.05 MB), its share of a 514² Float32
  elevation grid (1.06 MB, shared by up to 16 tiles) and a 33×33 mesh (~70 KB). 3DTRjs's cache
  charges every tile for the whole shared grid: 161 tiles counted 350 MB for 223 MB really held
  (169 MB imagery, 43 MB for 41 grids, 11 MB meshes).
- 3DTRjs shares one LRU tile cache (0.4 GiB) between every renderer, and a renderer whose cache is
  full requests nothing more. The terrain and imagery globe weighs ~2.2 MB a tile: measured over
  Bastia at 150 m, 161 globe tiles held 350 MB of the 429 and the dataset's 211 point tiles 66 MB.
  A pitched view or a long browse filled the cache with terrain and the data stopped at whatever it
  had, often the root. The globe renderers now have their own cache; the data renderer and its
  native-leaf children keep the default one to themselves.

### Fixed — 2026-10-29 (tile-server: S3 "socket hang up" no longer sticks)
- **Prod logs showed repeated `FetchError … socket hang up` from node-fetch inside the `copc` library**
  while the `open` mosaic loaded the DEFRA / NOAA overview COPCs. The errors were caught per request,
  so nothing crashed, but three things made one transient hang-up permanent until a restart:
  `memoizeAsync` cached a failed root load forever (the dataset answered 500 for the life of the
  process), `ensureForSubtree` / `drainAll` deleted a hierarchy page from `pendingPages` before
  reading it (a failed read lost the page and every subtree under it came back empty, silently), and
  a curved-placement tileset of a 900 km COPC drained its whole hierarchy with one unpooled,
  un-retried request per page (96 pages for DEFRA 2008) while the aggregate fanned out 16 such loads.
- **New `packages/tile-server/range-getter.js`** replaces the library's `Getter.http` for COPC: range
  reads issued in the same tick are sorted and coalesced when adjacent or less than 64 KB apart
  (capped at 8 MB per merged request), capped at 32 in flight per host (`TILED_RANGE_MAX_PER_HOST`),
  retried three times with backoff on resets / hang-ups / timeouts / 429 / 5xx, and any other non-2xx
  throws instead of being parsed as hierarchy or points. Measured: Liège overview, curved, 54 pages
  → 4 requests total (was 57); DEFRA 2008, 96 pages → 4 requests (was 99); Autzen implicit → 3.
- **`memoizeAsync` caches the promise** (concurrent first calls for one source share a single build)
  and takes `{ failureTtlMs }`; `copc-live` and `potree-live` pass 60 s, so a broken source still
  fails fast under polling but a hang-up during the root read heals by itself. Other adapters keep
  the permanent failure cache.
- **copc-live hierarchy pages are loaded through one in-flight map**: concurrent subtree requests
  share a page read instead of one of them answering with an empty block, a failed read puts the
  page back in `pendingPages`, and a failed `drainAll` resets so the next request finishes it.
  Verified by failing the first drain read three times: all 54 pages restored, next request loads
  them in one read. Background prefetches (`copc-live`, `potree-live`) now log failures instead of
  surfacing as `unhandledRejection` (the old `try { return promise } catch {}` caught nothing).

  "web-mercator quadtree" are not the same thing: Morton (Z-order) is an *addressing* scheme —
  interleave a cell's x/y bits — and says nothing about the projection. Bing's quadkey is the base-4
  Morton code of a **web-mercator** cell; 3D Tiles implicit tiling (and therefore Cesium) subdivides
  the **root bounding volume in its own coordinate space** — for a `region`, "`x`, `y`, and `z` refer
  to `longitude`, `latitude`, and `height`", bisected arithmetically — i.e. a **geodetic** quadtree
  (Cesium's `GeographicTilingScheme`, 2×1 cells at level 0) merely *indexed* with Morton codes. So
  Cesium's Morton order is **not** a mercator quadtree. Both schemes are now offered (`mercator`
  stays the `auto` default; `geodetic`/`morton`/`implicit`/`cesium` selects the other), and every
  group and dataset carries an `extras.cell` breadcrumb (`scheme`, path `key`, `level`, `morton`) so
  "why is this grouped here" is answerable from the tileset alone.
- **Fixed: `/aggregate` silently lost more than half its children at scale.** Firing all children at
  once with a plain `Promise.allSettled` is fine for 12 and broken for 500: a 500-dataset mosaic came
  back with **232 datasets and 268 bare `fetch failed` entries** — still answering `200`, because
  failed children degrade into `asset.extras.failed` by design. It looked fine and was missing half
  the world, and the survivors varied run to run, so the tree was not even reproducible. Child
  fetches now run through a **16-way pool**: 500/500, identical tree cold and warm.
- **Fixed: flat `/aggregate` roots violated the decrease-on-refine rule.** The flat root took
  `max(child GE)`, tying it to its largest child so both became selectable at the same SSE. Now ×2,
  matching what the nested path always did. Caught by the new test's monotonicity assertion.
- **Added: mosaic caching with a day-scale TTL** (`&ttl=`, default 86 400 s), keyed on the exact child
  set plus scheme; each child's fetched root is cached the same way, failures at 60 s so one dead host
  cannot pin a degraded mosaic for a day. `&ttl=0` bypasses the cache **in both directions** (an
  earlier pass skipped only the write, which still served stale data — the one thing the flag exists

### Added / Fixed — 2026-09-11b (aggregate spatial nesting · stats content sampling · Bing raster basemap)
- **Added: `/aggregate` spatial auto-nesting** (`&nest=auto`, on from 8 children). A flat root is fine
  for a dozen datasets and wrong for 500: every child sat directly under the root, so the moment the
  root refined, all 500 child `tileset.json` were candidates. Nesting builds a **web-mercator
  quadtree** from nothing but each child's root bounding sphere — the only thing knowable without
  opening the datasets — the same idea as 3D Tiles implicit tiling and Bing's quadkeys. Each dataset
  is placed at the **deepest level whose cell still comfortably contains it** (4× slack), so a 200 m
  scan lands in its city cell while a country-wide dataset stays shallow and is never split; the trie
  is then path-compressed, giving continent → country → city only where datasets actually diverge.
  Culling does the work at render time, but the **geometric error is what stops the fetch**: a group's
  GE is 2× its children's (also satisfying the spec's monotonic-decrease rule), so from a world view a
  site-sized group subtends a fraction of a pixel and is never traversed. Verified with 28 datasets
  worldwide — root fanout 28 → 4, depth 8, 20 groups, Paris nesting as Eiffel+Louvre (r≈2 km) →
  Sacré-Cœur (3 km) → Versailles (10 km), GE strictly monotonic. `&nest=0` restores the flat root.
- **Added: `/stats` content sampling** (`&sample=N`) plus `leafNodes`, per-level `leaves`, and
  `maxFanout`. The sampler picks N content tiles **stratified across depth** (leaves *and* intermediate
  nodes) and parses **only the GLB JSON chunk** — primitive counts, extensions, texture MIME types and
  compression are all declared there, so no Draco/meshopt/SPZ decode is needed. Off by default; the
  rest of the walk still reads nothing but tileset JSON and subtree buffers. Verified across content
  types: Potree → points, no triangles; Bing → 512 triangles / 1536 vertices / JPEG, exactly the globe
  shell's 16×16×2 grid; WL Elevator → ~24.7 k splats/tile with `KHR_gaussian_splatting` +
  `_compression_spz_2` + `KHR_materials_unlit` + `EXT_splat_opacity`.
- **Changed: the Bing globe shell now defaults to Bing's own raster tiles.** Low zoom was never a gap
  in `tf=3dv4` — it is a different product: Bing Maps itself draws imagery-on-terrain there and only
  switches to photogrammetry around z13. Those raster tiles are keyless, quadkey-addressed, and on the
  **same host** as the mesh, so the shell now matches what it hands over to. `&basemap=osm` and any
  `{z}/{x}/{y}` / `{q}` template remain available; attribution resolves from the *requested* basemap.
- **Fixed: two dead viewer splat presets.** The 3DGS-plugin repo renamed its samples
  (`data/gaussianSplat1|2` → `Elevator-3dtiles` / `SvirnasAlyt-3dtiles`); both preset URLs had been

### Fixed — 2026-07-07 (`main`: 3DTRJS wrong up-orientation on repeat Auto-zoom)
- **Root cause**: `frameToTileset` (`apps/viewer/src/components/Viewport/ThreeDTRjsViewer.tsx`) decides
  whether a tileset needs the synthetic ENU anchor (for non-georeferenced local content, e.g. Potree
  "Lion 2.0") by checking whether its bounding sphere — transformed by `tiles.group`'s CURRENT matrix —
  sits near world origin. Sound on the first call (fresh load), but the anchor branch then moves
  `tiles.group` onto the real ellipsoid, so any LATER call (e.g. clicking "Auto-zoom to tileset" again,
  with zero navigation in between) sees that same sphere near the real ellipsoid surface —
  indistinguishable from genuinely georeferenced content — and wrongly resets the rotation to identity
  while leaving the position at the anchor point. Reproduced in every controls mode (Globe/Orbit/
  Environment) since the bug lives entirely before any controls code runs.
- **Fix**: decide (and cache) whether a tileset needs the ENU anchor only once, on the fresh-load call
  (`frameToTileset` now takes an `isFreshLoad` flag), and reuse that cached decision on every later
  re-frame instead of re-deriving it from an already-moved sphere.
- A preceding, separate real bug was also fixed along the way: `GlobeControls`/`EnvironmentControls`
  (from `3d-tiles-renderer`) track a `zoomDirectionSet`/`zoomPoint` pair internally that's only ever
  cleared by real pointer input — after free-navigating far from a tileset and then hard-teleporting the
  camera back (`frameToTileset`), the controls' next tick re-raycast along that stale direction from the
  camera's new position and reoriented around a garbage point. `setControlsPivot` now optionally resets
  that state, used specifically by `frameToTileset`'s hard camera teleport.
- Verified live: Lion 2.0 stays upright across repeated Auto-zoom clicks in all three controls modes;
  real georeferenced content (COPC Autzen) is unaffected across the same repeat-click test.

### Fixed — rad-to-3dtiles (the "spiky render" saga)

  halving (`--ge-scale` / `--ge-layer`, default 1; an earlier ×16/×8 hack over-refined everything).

### Known limitations
- **Spherical harmonics** not yet emitted (DC only) on any path — the largest remaining fidelity gap.
- LCC scale decode (v3 geometric vs v5 codebook) and SH still being finalised.
- Tile conversion is **single-threaded / sequential** (see README "Performance"); a worker pool is the
  obvious next optimization.
